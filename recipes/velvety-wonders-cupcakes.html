Oven Magic

Velvety Wonders Cupcakes

The perfect batch when you're baking to make a difference — simple enough to make fifty without losing your mind, and good enough that nobody can tell it was made in a rush.

Prep
25 min
Bake
18–20 min
Makes
~12
Level
Medium

For the cupcakes

Tap each item as you go.

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • ¾ cup cocoa powder — unsweetened, so the chocolate flavour comes through
  • 1½ cups sugar
  • 1 cup milk — dairy-free milk works exactly the same
  • ½ cup melted butter — swap for oil if avoiding dairy
  • 2 eggs — or ¼ cup yogurt per egg for an eggless batch
  • 2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • A pinch of salt

For the ganache

  • 1 cup chocolate chips or chopped chocolate
  • ½ cup heavy cream — or full-fat coconut cream for a dairy-free swap

Method

  1. Prep the trays

    Preheat the oven and line your cupcake trays with liners — as many trays as you'll actually need, prepped before you start mixing.

  2. Dry mix

    Whisk the flour, cocoa powder, sugar, baking powder, and salt together in one bowl.

  3. Wet mix

    In another bowl, mix the milk, melted butter, eggs (or yogurt, if going eggless), and vanilla.

  4. Combine

    Combine the wet and dry ingredients and stir just until smooth — don't overmix, especially if you're rushing a big batch.

  5. Fill & bake

    Fill each liner about two-thirds full. Bake until a toothpick comes out clean, checking a little earlier if you're baking several trays at once — ovens don't always behave the same with more in them.

  6. Cool completely

    Let them cool completely before touching the ganache step — warm cupcakes just melt it right off.

  7. Make the ganache

    Warm the cream until it's just about to simmer, then pour it over the chocolate and let it sit for a minute before stirring until smooth and glossy.

  8. Top & set

    Let the ganache cool slightly so it thickens a little, then spoon or dip each cupcake top into it. Let the ganache set before boxing them up to give away.

Vedhica's note: baking for a cause isn't about getting it perfect — it's about getting it done, with care, even when things go a little wrong. Nobody who receives a cupcake knows about the tray that didn't rise. They just know somebody thought of them.
